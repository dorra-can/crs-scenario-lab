Start hereHow to use this tool▾
1

Set your baseline

On the left, under Held constant, set every answer to your situation (age, education, language, work experience, and so on). The big number is your baseline CRS score, with its breakdown below it. This is the profile every scenario starts from.

2

Choose what to vary

In Step 2 — Factors to vary, flip on any factor you want to test, then tick which values to include (for example Age → 28, 29, 30, 31, 32). Anything left off keeps its baseline value. Turn on several factors to compare every combination at once.

3

Compare the results

The table builds one row per combination, showing the total CRS, the change vs. your baseline, and every sub-score. Click any column heading to sort, and use Export CSV to copy the results into Excel or Google Sheets.

Example
“Does one more year of Canadian work experience beat improving my French?” Set your baseline, turn on Canadian work experience and Second language — CLB, tick the values you're weighing, and read the two effects side by side in the table.
  • Bringing a spouse can lower your score — switch Application type to “With accompanying spouse” to see it; your own core points drop, and the spouse's own credentials have to make up the difference.
  • Provincial nomination adds 600 points — turn it on to see the effect an invitation from a province would have.
  • Your setup is saved in this browser, so it's still here when you come back. Use Reset variations to clear the factors you're varying.
  • Scores mirror the official IRCC calculator, but this is a planning tool — confirm any real application against the official calculator.

Point tables & how the score is built▾

Total CRS = Core / human-capital (age, education, official languages, Canadian work experience) + Spouse factors + Skill transferability (education×language, education×Canadian experience, foreign experience×language/Canadian experience, certificate of qualification) + Additional points. Columns marked “with spouse” apply when an accompanying spouse is included; that path lowers the core caps but adds up to 40 spouse points.

Notable rules the tool reproduces: language points depend on CLB level, not the specific test (with one exception — TCF Canada at CLB 4 scores 0 for the first language). The French bonus pays 50 only when English is the first language at CLB 5+ and French is second at CLB 7+; French as the first language pays 25. Arranged-employment / job-offer points are 0 since 25 March 2025.
